Budgeting 101
The Basics

What Is the 50/30/20 Budget Rule?

Popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in her book All Your Worth, the 50/30/20 rule is a simple framework for allocating your after-tax income:

  • 50% to Needs — Essentials you can't live without
  • 30% to Wants — Things that make life enjoyable
  • 20% to Savings & Debt Repayment — Building your future

The beauty of this system is its simplicity. No complicated spreadsheets, no tracking every penny. Just three big buckets. But simplicity is also its biggest trap — beginners often misclassify expenses or set unrealistic percentages.

How To

How to Calculate Your 50/30/20 Budget

Start with your after-tax (take-home) income — what lands in your bank account after taxes, Social Security, Medicare, and pre-tax deductions.

Step 1: Find Your Monthly Take-Home Pay

If salaried, divide annual take-home by 12. If hourly, multiply weekly average by 4.33.

Step 2: Apply the Percentages

Multiply your monthly take-home by 0.50, 0.30, and 0.20.

Step 3: Categorize Your Current Spending

List everything you spent last month. Put each expense into one of the three buckets. Be honest — that daily latte is a "want," not a "need."

Avoid These

Common Budgeting Mistakes Beginners Make

  • Misclassifying wants as needs. Your gym membership and premium Spotify are wants. Be honest.
  • Ignoring irregular expenses. Car insurance, annual subscriptions, gifts — divide by 12 and set aside monthly.
  • The all-or-nothing trap. Overspent this month? Adjust next month. Consistency beats perfection.
  • No emergency fund first. Prioritize 3-6 months of expenses before aggressive investing. See our emergency fund guide.
Customize

Adjusting the 50/30/20 Rule for Your Situation

  • High-cost city: Needs may hit 55-60%. Reduce wants to 25-30%.
  • High debt: Divert 10% from wants to debt. 50/20/30.
  • High income ($150k+): Push savings to 30%. 50/20/30.
  • Retirement catch-up (50+): Aim for 25% savings. 50/25/25.

How to Automate Your Budget

  • Set up direct deposit splitting — send 20% directly to savings.
  • Automate bill payments from needs and wants accounts.
  • Use a budgeting app. Check our budgeting 101 guide for recommendations.
Get Started

Getting Started Today

  • Calculate your monthly take-home pay
  • Review last month's spending — categorize every expense
  • Set up budget buckets: needs checking, wants checking, savings
  • Automate your savings first (pay yourself before paying bills)
  • Track for 30 days and adjust
The 50/30/20 budget rule won't make you a millionaire overnight. But it will give you something more valuable — control over your money. And in 2026, with inflation still running, that control matters more than ever.
